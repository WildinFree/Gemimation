# Worked beat: a runaway bell

This is an invented illustration of direction. Its words and timings are examples, not a script to substitute for the user's. A real production replaces them with the supplied script and measured recording.

Suppose a phrase says: "One tiny bell woke the whole harbour." The stressed "bell" begins at 1.20 s, "woke" at 2.00 s and "harbour" at 3.00 s.

## Identity and camera idea

A young harbour messenger has a long egg-shaped head, narrow upright torso, orange work apron over navy clothes, a short uneven fringe and a forward-leaning eager posture. A shorter, wide square-headed night watchkeeper has a bell-shaped coat, tired half-lidded eyes and a deliberate sideways lean. Both use round ink hands and line limbs. Their silhouettes differ without headwear.

Camera idea: ride the vibration from a tiny bell to a huge consequence. Signature warm source: the messenger's lantern against a deep blue harbour. The hook is an already swinging bell; the turn is an absurd chain of awakening objects; the payoff is the watchkeeper's exhausted delayed reaction. The script decides which of these beats belong.

## First four seconds

| Time | Picture, camera and sound |
|---|---|
| 0.00-0.75 | Begin mid-swing. A round hand grips a short rope visibly connected to the bell. The camera tracks its arc with a blurred near beam framing one edge. Small anticipatory metal creak; no title. |
| 0.75-1.03 | Travel toward the bell's clapper. Keep the hand/rope relationship readable. The camera reaches the detail about 0.17 s before "bell." |
| 1.03-1.33 | A tiny creep maintains life while the clapper finishes its arc. Contact lands at 1.17 s, slightly before the stressed word. One horizontal highlight, small tapered ring and the bell strike share the contact frame. |
| 1.33-2.75 | Follow the force outward in a continuous curved pullback. Foreground rigging moves faster than distant roofs. Keep one readable consequence at a time; show it beginning on "woke," rather than cutting repeatedly. |
| 2.75-4.00 | The harbour becomes clear before "harbour." Read its scale while the camera continues a slow rise. Lanterns and hanging ropes respond with a small delay; the watchkeeper realizes what happened only after looking toward the source. |

The same principle can be executed with generated video, drawn animation, compositing or code. The specified outcome is the spatial/timing relationship, not a software recipe.

## Clip generation brief

Use the current film's character reference and palette. Start with the bell already swinging and the rope taut in the messenger's round hand. No fingers. Keep the night watchkeeper short and broad with a square head. Use one continuous move from rope/hand to clapper, then along the vibration into a wider harbour reveal. The clapper contacts at 1.17 s; keep the mechanism visible. End after 4.00 s on the wide harbour composition with upward camera momentum. No extra dialogue, subtitles, signs, text or camera teleport. Preserve the lantern's warm pool and blue distance.

If tools only produce a shorter segment, use a boundary with a stable grip and compatible camera velocity. Provide the last generated frame and exact outgoing state to the next segment, then inspect both sides for identity and timing drift.

## What would fail

An unattached rope, gripping fingers, silent contact, a camera arriving after the word, a glow on the whole face, three disconnected close-up cuts, a static wide, a sudden lighting change or bells copied from the studies all miss the brief.
