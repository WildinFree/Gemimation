# Camera and composition studies

These five sheets are private visual study material supplied with the pack. Use them to understand framing, depth, camera journeys, scale changes, light-led attention and scene handoffs. Do not publish the study images in the finished film or copy their characters, stories, props, wording or exact shots. The license for this pack's instructions does not relicense third-party reference images.

**The written guides define the target character design and acting.** These sheets contain a different character style, photographic detail and burned-in text. Ignore those features: do not reproduce captions, signage, faces or costumes from the studies.

Each sheet has 60 sampled images in a 10-column by 6-row grid. Read left to right, then top to bottom. Grid positions show sequence, not consecutive video frames. Static sheets cannot prove exact duration, camera acceleration, synchronization or an invisible edit. Use the guide timing bands for planning and actual output playback for verification.

The sheets preserve the existing study files unchanged; neutral names describe the lesson, not a production asset.

## A useful reading exercise

1. Pick a row and mark the point the eye follows across its panels.
2. Compare the foreground, subject plane and far distance: what tells you the camera moved?
3. Find a detail that only becomes meaningful when the next wider framing appears.
4. Identify a stable shape or direction that could link two scenes.
5. Invent a different shot for the supplied script using that principle.

Carry the relationship into your own scene, not the pictured contents. Use [the camera guide](../guides/02-storyboard-and-camera.md) for motion/timing and [the transition guide](../guides/04-transitions-and-continuity.md) for handoff design. Their current creative rules take precedence over techniques merely visible in a study.
