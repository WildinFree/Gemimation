# Animated-Shorts-Gemini

Turn the supplied script and recorded audio into an appealing, coherent animated video. Preserve the creative intent while choosing your own production methods. The package supplies direction, not an execution environment.

## Inputs and output

Treat the script as the narrative source and the audio as the timing master. Preserve wording, order, voice, performance and voice level. Do not invent narration, topics or dialogue. If they differ, identify the mismatch and resolve it before making irreversible timing choices. Visual gags may elaborate what is said without changing its meaning.

Default to 9:16, 30 fps and a native 1440 x 2560 final picture where available. Runtime follows the supplied audio and a short purposeful tail; 55-70 seconds is typical, not a requirement to stretch or truncate the recording. Start visually at time zero. No intro, logo, outro, end card, captions, subtitles or explanatory labels. Readable text within the scene requires the user's request.

Deliver the actual video with synchronized supplied audio when your tools can produce it. Check your available capabilities at the start. Use image/video generation, compositing, animation, audio or code tools as appropriate; no particular framework, renderer or installed assets are required. Prefer methods that give the best control over identity, motion and timing.

If a required production capability is absent, state that specific limit early, continue the work you can perform and deliver the strongest usable artifact: finished clips, storyboard frames, a timed animatic, renderable animation, or exact generation/editing instructions with the cue map. Clearly distinguish these from a finished video. Never claim to have rendered, listened to, measured or inspected an artifact you could not access.

## Creative contract

- **Look:** expressive flat cartoon characters in dimensional, softly shaded cartoon environments. Plum ink, simple line limbs, round hands, saturated colours, coloured shadows, coherent materials and grounded contact.
- **Identity:** design fresh people for this story. Choose silhouettes, faces, complete clothing and motivated habits. Preserve each person's design across all generated images and clips. Prior assets and prior projects are unnecessary.
- **Story:** start mid-action with a readable cause and effect. Plan hook, turn and payoff before decorative detail.
- **Camera:** one story-specific camera idea; moving takes with purposeful framing, readable arrivals, parallax and quiet creep between major moves.
- **Editing:** travel between framings in the same space. Connect changes of place/time through motion, matches, real occlusion or short moving blends.
- **Performance:** thought precedes action. Use anticipation, contact, reaction, overlap and settle; speech has expressive syllable shapes, not a repetitive mouth flap.
- **Sound:** preserve the voice. Add supportive sound and music only where available and appropriate, synchronized to visible events.
- **Quality:** inspect the actual result, correct faults and preserve continuity through revisions. Do not substitute a detailed plan for a video when production tools are available.

## Production sequence

1. **Listen and map.** Establish duration, phrases, stressed words, pauses and punchlines from the actual recording. If audio inspection is unavailable, use supplied timing data provisionally and do not claim to know its stress or syllables. Mark uncertain timings. Resolve only questions that block meaningful work.
2. **Direct.** Choose story-specific character identities, palette, setting, camera idea and three hero moments. Write a compact beat map tied to audio time.
3. **Test the look.** Check the cast in multiple views, silhouettes, resting faces and key reactions; check a representative lit scene and hero prop. Fix foundational faults before producing the whole film.
4. **Build the moving story.** Make complete actions and moving takes. For separate generated clips, specify matching start/end states, a shared design description and transition overlap. Anchor important events to exact audio cues.
5. **Finish sound and loop.** Add synchronized effects and a subordinate music bed after picture timing is stable. For a complete replaying short, join the ending to the opening with matched composition and motion. For a requested excerpt, preserve its entry/exit continuity and leave room for the surrounding film instead of forcing a loop.
6. **Review and repair.** Check the entire available video and all frames you can access, then inspect risky moments at full resolution. Recheck changed sequences and their neighbours. Report what remains unchecked.

Keep one compact continuity record: character descriptions, scale, palette, set geography, prop state, hand ownership, light, camera direction and audio cues. It belongs to this film and prevents generation drift. Do not create unrelated project administration or require the user to approve every ordinary decision.

## Decision priority

User instructions and supplied story facts come first. The written creative guides define the target look and craft. Reference images supply camera/composition lessons only. Numbers are practical starting bands; perceptual clarity, audio meaning and story intent decide exceptions. Never impose a statistical quota when it would make a shot worse.
