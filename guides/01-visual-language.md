# Visual language

## The picture

Combine flat, graphic cartoon characters with dimensional cartoon props and environments. Preserve the contrast between simple drawn people and a richly staged world, while unifying both through ink, palette, soft coloured shading and physical contact.

The world is storybook soft: clean local colour, a flat lit core rolling gently into lavender shade, friendly rounded edges and slightly chunky proportions. Avoid hard two-tone bands, photographic grain, photoreal people, faceted close-up geometry and a uniform plastic shine. Mood changes through lighting, atmosphere and palette; the underlying drawing language stays consistent.

Use deep plum ink, approximately #241726, instead of pure black. Outer contours are smooth, antialiased and heavier on the side away from the light. Character outlines are about 5% of head width with a roughly 3:1 thick/thin variation; internal facial lines are lighter, clothing seams lighter again. Environment silhouettes are approximately 4-5 pixels at a 1080-pixel-wide design, fine creases 1.5-2 pixels. Scale screen-sized details with output resolution. Avoid double outlines, stray dashes, jagged edges and dark lines across every surface joint.

## Character design

Design identity from role, age, situation, desire and personality. One dominant shape idea leads each person; the other shapes support it. Contrast head and torso shapes rather than making a stack of matching pears or circles.

Head vocabulary: bean, egg, softened square, long, wedge, bulb, bell or an asymmetric rounded form. Avoid a featureless perfect circular head. No visible neck. Use subtle asymmetry toward the facing side, and shift facial features about 11% of head width in a three-quarter view while tucking the far eye toward the nose.

A useful adult baseline is 3.5 head heights: head 1, torso 1.1, legs 1.4. Children are about 2.7-3 head heights. Stout adults have wider torsos and slightly shorter legs. Vary height, width, head-to-body ratio and posture across the cast. Keep those proportions stable in every view and shot.

Give each main character a different combination of at least two strong silhouette cues: body shape, height/build, head/hair outline, signature garment/prop, or posture. A height difference around 15% is a useful cue, but clear width and shape differences can do the job. Extras remain less distinctive and do not borrow a main's signature silhouette.

Shape associations are options: soft round forms can feel friendly; squares sturdy; points dangerous; tall thin forms nervous or officious. Express status, mood and intention through clothes, posture and performance rather than skin tone. Cast roles across a broad skin-tone range; retain eye and mouth readability on deep skin.

### Faces

Use clear graphic eyes, brows, nose and a small resting mouth. No blush, glossy catchlights or realistic eyelashes. Approximate eye spacing is 26% of head width. Shock eyes can enlarge to white discs around 22% of head width with small pupils. Brows are substantial, roughly 17-18% of head width long. A resting mouth is a short dash around 9-14% of head width; mouth interior #5a1e28, tongue #e86e78, sweat #9fd4ff.

Those are proportions, not a demand to make every face identical. Choose rest eye shape, spacing, brow, nose, hair and posture for the individual. Keep eyes, mouth and brows attached to the face through turns and exaggeration. In profile, show a readable nose and compressed far-side features.

Show a resting expression plus at least three story-relevant reactions for every main. Useful vocabulary includes listening, delight, scheming, smugness, shock, jaw-drop, panic, worry, embarrassment, cringe, grief, anger, fuming, eye-roll, suspicion and deadpan. Select what the beat means. Big reactions should be legible even without dialogue.

### Limbs and hands

All people have simple continuous plum-ink line limbs, round ends and whole circular hands. Arms are smooth hose curves with no drawn elbow, wrist or shoulder joint. Legs are nearly straight at rest with rounded bends in action. No fingers, thumbs, knuckles, nails, coloured sleeves drawn as limbs, tapered limbs or shiny hands, including in extreme close-up.

Relative to head width: arm stroke about 5.2%, leg stroke 5.8%, hand radius 5.5%, short foot length 16%, foot thickness 7.5%. Preserve these relationships when enlarged. Feet turn out slightly in front view and point forward in profile. A relaxed A-stance places feet about 45% of head width apart.

Limbs keep their lengths during movement; apparent shortening comes from perspective and foreshortening. Adult arm length is roughly 0.95 head heights. Draw the rear arm behind the torso. Draw an active front arm in front; for a grip, layer arm, prop, then the hand circle over its grip point so the object visibly continues on both sides.

### Wardrobe and appeal

Design the entire visible outfit: garment silhouette, layers, lower garment, footwear and necessary headwear/accessories. Aim for five or fewer clear identifying elements. A new hat alone cannot supply an identity. Use one hero colour with quieter base cloth where the setting allows; documented dress follows its real palette. Dark clothes remain coloured darks lighter than the outline.

Headwear must fit the actual head and remain attached in front, profile, back and motion. Keep faces readable under brims. If the subject is a real event, check visible period/place details using reliable available references; do not invent a historical detail with false certainty.

## Colour and light

Use saturated local colours against quieter backgrounds. Example day anchors: red #e85648, greens #7cbb48/#58af47, sky blue #8fc4fa, muted blue #6d76aa. Darks are navy #262c51, aubergine #2e2439, plum #3d2530 or oxblood #4d2725. Shadows retain colour and separation.

| Mood | Direction |
|---|---|
| Day | warm side/back key, cool coloured fill, blue distance; readable hue in shadow |
| Morning | low peach key, pale blue sky, long soft shadows |
| Warm interior | warm pools against cool fill; a visible lamp or window motivates the key |
| Golden | orange-peach backlight, violet-blue replacement shadows, coral/pink sky, long shadows |
| Overcast | soft grey-blue light, weak short shadows, restrained contrast |
| Night | deep blue ambient with one signature warm action pool and small distant hot lights |
| Dream/fear | violet distance and a strong red-orange key, close atmospheric layers |
| Memory | a restrained cooler or softened palette, with the same material/drawing style |

Build light in layers: readable ambient base, one dominant key, coloured fill, story practicals, contact/cast shadows, atmosphere, then final grade. Do not let competing shadow directions make one place incoherent. Keep the perceived key stable through a take; any changing light has a story cause.

A useful day shadow/lit display-luma ratio on one surface is 0.45-0.55. Golden shade replaces orange-brown with violet-blue; it is not simply a darker orange. No crushed black shade, grey unlit faces or lemon/lavender skin highlights. Maintain each person's skin identity under every mood. Brightness targets from pale reference faces do not mean bleaching deep skin.

Faces stay clear with a gentle side/top gradient; torsos can shade more strongly, down to roughly 55-66% of their lit value. People take the scene's tint and shadow without looking like self-lit stickers. Put small light accents inside silhouettes; avoid an offset outer rim that makes a hat look framed.

Lamps, candles, fire and magic must illuminate nearby objects as well as glow. Fade changes over roughly 0.33-0.5 s, minimum 0.13 s unless a motivated flash. Keep normal flicker slow and shallow. Air can carry quiet dust, mist or moths around the lit area. One signature warm source owns the warmest colour.

### Depth, sky and water

Separate foreground, action plane and distance. Haze pulls far surfaces and their outlines toward the horizon colour; it must not fog out the main action. Use a bright, pale horizon grading to saturated blue by day, peach through coral/pink/lilac at sunset, or deep blue at night. Avoid a grey strip between warm sky colours.

Clouds have a flat base, uneven rounded lobes and three soft tones: warm crown, midtone, lavender-blue underside. They generally have no heavy ink outline.

Use selective depth of field on close/medium/inserts, roughly one third of screen time. Keep wide geography readable and the speaking face sharp. Blur a near framing object more than the action plane; never smear the whole frame. Focus changes support a motivated camera move rather than pulsing independently. Glow belongs to light emitters and true highlights, never to white faces or bright walls.

Water is a saturated gradient, lighter/cyan toward the horizon and deeper near the lens. Use sparse horizontal crests with a lighter top and dark trough, thinning toward the foreground. No photographic mirrored reflections or vertical texture streaks. Sunset water can take sky colour and small warm light patches while staying saturated.

## Materials, places and props

Give each material a distinct response: matte cloth/straw, soft raw wood, a satin painted surface, glossy china/enamel, reflective metal, restrained glass and genuinely luminous sources. Metal needs broad light/dark reflection bands and a highlight; flat grey does not read as metal. Polished chrome is sharper than brass; iron is softer. Avoid sparkling noise and stock flares.

Near objects have rounded edges, thick readable rims/handles and joined moving parts. Do not reveal low-detail geometry in a macro. Architecture has depth: window recesses, sills, frames, eaves, steps, thick doors, trim and selective wear. Avoid plain boxes with flat window stickers. Establish period with a few unmistakable material/silhouette cues.

Spend effort by maximum screen importance:

| Tier | Guideline | Approximate effort share |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | any plot object, or at least 25% of frame height | 50% |
| Supporting piece | at least 8% | 30% |
| Dressing | at least 2% | 15% |
| Distance | smaller | 5% |

These prioritize effort, not software construction. Far scenery may simplify into hazy silhouettes; hero mechanisms need readable working parts.

Make locations lived in through clustered large/medium/small detail, about 1:3:6, across three depth bands. Rich scenes can support 30+ prop kinds and around 80% occupied cells in a 6-by-10 composition grid. Treat these as richness diagnostics, never a reason to fill deliberate negative space. Add three place-specific details, one or two quiet background jokes and at least two subtle ambient movers where appropriate. Wear belongs at contact/use points: chipped edges, patched plaster, base grime, a crooked shutter. Preserve one dominant action and no more than about three attention centres.
