# Transitions and continuity

## One continuous ride

A place/time normally holds one moving take. Travel between its subjects. When place or time changes, let direction, attention, an object, a real opening or motivated foreground occlusion introduce the next scene.

A moving cross-blend normally lasts 0.2-0.45 s (6-14 frames at 30 fps; about nine frames is a good first test). Both views continue moving in compatible directions. Align the subject of attention before blending; moving two unrelated pictures through each other is not a seamless transition.

Reserve hard cuts for motion/graphic matches, cut-on-action, real occlusion, a whip or a motivated impact. Avoid obvious hard cuts in the opening stretch. A dissolve signals a meaningful time/place jump, usually around 0.27-0.4 s, and matches composition or shape.

Place a transition centre within about 0.067 s of its anchor word, unless a deliberate pause is the joke. Keep the next subject near the viewer's current attention point. A useful eye-trace jump ceiling is 12% of the frame's shorter dimension; use normalized screen positions rather than world coordinates to compare.

No long fades, fades to black/white, end cards, page turns, spins, star wipes, sliding frames or generic zoom-blur presets. A flash is motivated by an impact/lightning and lasts no more than about 0.067 s. Camera zooms, orbits and pass-throughs are actual perspective/motion changes, not a transition preset concealing absent staging.

## Practical handoffs

- **Connected spaces:** fly through a door, along the route or over an obstacle. Maintain plausible geography rather than introducing an edit.
- **Through a story object:** approach a visible opening until it occupies the lens, then emerge from an aligned opening in the next place. Match direction, scale progression and attention. Never expose the inside of a solid wall.
- **Match:** place the shared object/shape at the same screen position and compatible size before the change.
- **Time passes:** retain the camera journey and scene layout while sky, shadows and practical lights change. A soft moving light boundary or matched dissolve can carry the passage. Fade lights naturally rather than switching every lamp on in one frame.
- **Foreground occlusion:** a real body, post, door or other scene object covers roughly 90% of the picture; place the hidden change under maximum cover.
- **Whip:** accelerate in/out over about 0.07-0.1 s with strong directional blur at the change. Incoming motion continues the outgoing direction. Whips are rare accents, not the default for every sentence.
- **Impact flash:** the action motivates a very short flash, a contact sound and a kick on the same event.

Sound reinforces the handoff at its centre without announcing an arbitrary transition. Keep visible effects as part of the story; do not invent a decorative cover just because an edit is difficult.

## Loop ending

Apply this ending to a complete replaying short. A requested excerpt needs continuity with its surrounding film, not its own artificial loop.

The replay should feel like the next beat. The ending can cause the opening, repeat its pattern or rhyme with its subject, scale, screen position and camera motion.

Leave enough time for the final reaction: a moving reading interval of at least about 0.7 s before an approximately 0.3 s loop handoff. Plan its arrival roughly one second before the end. Extend a short visual tail if necessary, preserving the supplied audio; do not rush the payoff or slow the voice to manufacture this space.

Match the ending with the opening's approach, not only its isolated first frame. Compare subject position, scale, gaze, motion direction, light and dominant shapes at the middle of the join. Double images mean the cameras or actors have drifted apart.

If a blend helps, use overlapping opening motion before time zero so the actual first frame continues that motion naturally. This pre-roll is visual; it must not duplicate or alter the voice. If a precise match is impossible, prefer a motivated action/occlusion handoff over a ghosting dissolve.

No outro chord or music fade. Music continues to the final frame; choose an audio seam that avoids a click where tools permit. Preserve the narration's end and do not overlap intelligible words across the seam. Watch/listen to end -> beginning at least twice.

## Continuity and scene integrity

Keep a compact state table for changing characters/objects: position, facing, screen side, hand ownership, damage, dirt/wetness, light, weather and count. Once a thing breaks, it stays broken until a visible repair occurs.

People retain height/build, face, hair, clothes and personal movement across takes. Props retain dimensions, colour and materials. For a new generated segment, provide the expected entry state and outgoing reference frame, then verify the actual result instead of trusting the prompt.

Ground feet and object bases. Every contact has a believable support/shadow. Held props, hats, rider/mount, sitter/seat, hinges and wheels move with their physical relationships. Limbs and props do not detach at a view change.

Allow clearance for flat character representations to rotate toward the lens during an orbit; avoid walls cutting their edges. Lying/rolled figures need a stable orientation. Keep overlapping planes at distinct depths, or use consistent intentional compositing, to prevent flicker and depth-order swaps. Offset ground effects enough to avoid fighting with the floor, without making them float.

Check moving routes and camera paths between key poses, not just at their ends. The lens cannot pass through a solid surface or reveal an unfinished interior. Stage crowds in staggered depth with clear personal space. Background people stay quieter than the main action. Figures, dust, rings and smoke respect surfaces and ceilings.

A complex shot is built/reviewed in layers: set, props, people, moving objects, effects. Prefer fewer large readable elements over noisy detail when clarity fails.
