# Storyboard and camera

## Begin with meaning and the recording

For each phrase identify what the viewer must understand: a cause, an object, a change, a consequence or a reaction. Write picture beats against the actual audio, then decide how the camera reveals them. Explanations become close physical demonstrations of mechanisms, not labelled diagrams.

Choose one camera idea that belongs to this particular story: follow its object through successive hands; ride a force through a mechanism; climb from cellar to roof; move from tiny cause to enormous consequence; retrace a journey in reverse; or pass through the story object at each place change. A watcher idea still has a quiet moving vantage and earns its major reveal.

Mark three hero moments: hook, turn and payoff. Each earns a deliberate camera event on its important word or visual cue. Audio stress must be heard or supplied explicitly; timestamped text alone does not establish it. Keep the intervening moves quieter so those events register.

## Cold open

Time zero is already mid-action: a physical, dramatic or funny cause and effect before the viewer knows the whole location. It must read with sound muted.

Strong patterns include an approaching threat behind an oblivious person, an impossible object behaviour, a small cause creating an outsized consequence, or a follow-camera ending on impact. Invent the action for the supplied script; do not borrow a reference story.

Aim for action immediately, a close mechanism/detail by roughly one second, and the wider context by two to three seconds. These are three readable framings, ideally within a continuous journey, not a requirement for three hard cuts. Do not compress so many actions into the opening that none can be read.

## Beat map and storyboard

For each take record:

- Start/end time and exact audio phrase or anchor word.
- What is already happening at entry; the key pose, contact, reveal and exit state.
- Subject of attention, its screen position, scale and clear background.
- Camera path, moving rest/arrival, reason for moving, light and focus.
- Character expressions, gaze, gestures, hand/prop ownership and sound cues.
- How it joins adjacent takes and any likely clipping/identity/timing risk.

Storyboard the opening, every major camera arrival, the three hero moments, each scene handoff and the loop. Add boards where a complex action or mechanism is ambiguous. Show what changes between boards, not merely a row of attractive stills. The [worked example](../examples/worked-beat.md) demonstrates this level of specificity.

For generated clips, define the initial state and required final state, the complete action between them, timing anchors and permitted camera motion. Keep a concise consistent identity description and approved visual samples for the current film. Shared descriptions maintain continuity; no previous project or asset library is needed.

## Moving takes and pacing

One place/time normally forms one moving take. Reframe by travelling, not cutting between every speaking face or noun. A framing arrival is a station within a shot; it is not itself an edit.

Aim around three readable stations per ten seconds, normally with at least 0.6 s of reading time at each. Include a motivated 3-7 s travel about every fifteen seconds when duration and story allow it. Broadly, three or four distinct takes per thirty seconds is enough; avoid place/time changes more often than roughly once per eight seconds. These are pacing bands: a short recording or a necessary story jump may justify a different count.

Dialogue can use 1-2 s reframing moves between faces. A long two-shot needs changing expression and intention. Physical journeys get one sustained follow rather than repeated miniature hops. Alternate fast discovery, legible action and a held reaction. If a moment is dull, improve staging, pose or timing before making it longer.

### Arrival and rest

The camera arrives about 0.17 s before an important word, giving the viewer time to see the subject before hearing it named. Maintain subtle push, creep, drift or a slow orbit during the reading interval. Avoid handheld float and aimless sinusoidal wobble. A quiet movement has a destination and never loses its subject.

A standard reframe usually takes roughly 0.6-1.3 s, smoothly accelerating and decelerating. A quiet push may increase subject scale around 4% per second; a punch push about 20% over a second. Keep these subordinate to readability. Do not accelerate a large journey into a three-frame unblurred teleport just to hit a cue.

As a screen-space starting point at a 720-pixel-wide reference, ordinary glide peaks are around 14-16 pixels per frame at 30 fps, usually below 25; quiet drift is much smaller. Scale pixel values with image width and speed per frame inversely with frame rate. Those are diagnostics, not substitute camera paths. Strong parallax and subject tracking may look alive even when translation measurements are small.

Rolls belong inside moves, usually 4-6 degrees, and settle level. Reserve stronger rolls for danger/falls; a static tilted shot is exceptional. Impact kicks are brief alternating offsets that decay in about 0.2-0.27 s, not a long soft wobble. Ordinary camera movement continues except for a one- or two-frame impact stop.

## Composition for a phone

Stage in depth and vertically; a 9:16 frame gives a sideways traveller little room. Use lead room in its direction of motion and anticipate where it will exit. When following a subject, near scenery must stream at a visibly different speed from far scenery.

One rest frames one subject for one reason. The viewer should find it in under half a second. Use silhouette, value contrast, focus, gaze, moving light or motion to guide attention. Avoid competing faces, unnecessary clutter and tangents.

Practical starting points:

| Choice | Starting range |
|---|---|
| Ordinary lens | approximately 34-46 degrees vertical field of view; moderate perspective |
| Lens height with people | chest to eye level, or a deliberately low power angle |
| High/top-down | geography and objects; descend as a person becomes the subject |
| Horizon | around 40-50% of frame height |
| Foreground framing | around one third of medium/close shots, occupying 10-25% of an edge |
| Near framing depth | roughly one third to one half of the subject's distance |

A reference shot mix was about 3% close, 27% medium, 28% full, 12% wide, 8% extreme wide and 22% inserts. Use its lesson: explanations and context both matter. Do not force those percentages onto a script.

Headroom changes with pose and camera movement. Let an intentional extreme lean crop part of a head, but never accidentally crop a hand, the object being explained or the action's contact. Keep most action within the central 80% of visible width. Shoot crowd geography from a viewpoint that preserves the main's silhouette.

## Camera vocabulary

Choose the move because of what the viewer learns.

| Move | Purpose and execution |
|---|---|
| Push/pull | reveal importance or context by travelling closer/farther; retain depth cues |
| Orbit/arc | expose relationships around a subject; preserve geography and clear backgrounds |
| Track/truck | travel beside action with differential near/far motion |
| Crane reveal | rise from a detail to a place or descend from geography to a person |
| Macro insert | show the physical mechanism, interior or changing part with dramatic light |
| Follow-camera | ride a spark, rolling object or projectile and arrive at its consequence |
| Chase | low behind a runner or vehicle; keep the intended route readable |
| Vertigo drop | tilt/fall along a shaft or cliff to communicate depth; land attention clearly |
| POV/frame-in-frame | see through a windscreen, door or opening with foreground context |
| Push-through | cross a real opening into connected space or an aligned scene handoff |
| Over-shoulder | connect an actor's intention with the thing they affect |
| Worm's-eye | foreground at ground height, a large arrival towering above |
| Lens-lean | an expressive face approaches a wide-feeling lens, filling 40-60% of the frame |
| Peek-up | a head/object rises from the bottom edge on a reveal |
| Snap zoom | a rare 0.07-0.13 s punch into an eye or realisation, followed by readable creep |
| Catch | camera momentum continues, then turns after a departing subject with 0.08-0.15 s aim lag |
| Momentum stop | following camera carries on 0.2-0.33 s after a stop, then settles back |
| Follow-away | track a departing subject while narrowing the view so it shrinks more slowly |

On a named-object punch: push in over roughly 0.33-0.5 s, let the object act about 0.07 s after arrival, read it for 0.5-1.05 s, then return toward the face over 0.27-0.47 s. If it is huge or distant, keep context instead of forcing a macro.

For a mechanism, follow the force in one journey: hand, lever, rod, consequence. For a jump/throw, catch the takeoff, move with the arc, put its apex on the stressed word, then follow down to contact. For an impact, approach its point, contact just before the word, kick, and pull back to show the aftermath. For a clash, orbit the shared action and save the strongest push for the deciding blow.
